# FMCG Consumer Behavior — Word-of-Mouth Research Study

## Overview

A mixed-methods academic research project examining the role of informal communication (word-of-mouth) in the purchase decisions of consumers under 21 years old within the Fast-Moving Consumer Goods (FMCG) sector.

Completed as part of the Marketing Research course at Kozminski University (Warsaw, 2025).

---

## Objective

To understand how peer recommendations, family advice, and online reviews influence everyday purchasing decisions among young consumers — and what this means for FMCG marketing strategy.

---

## Research Questions

1. How much do consumers under 21 rely on informal recommendations when buying FMCG products?
2. Which sources of recommendation (friends, family, influencers, online reviews) carry the most trust?
3. Does negative purchase experience increase future dependence on word-of-mouth?
4. Which product categories are most influenced by peer recommendations?

---

## Methodology

### Qualitative Phase — Focus Groups
- 14 participants (primarily under 21)
- Semi-structured discussion guide
- Thematic analysis of transcripts
- Goal: uncover motivations, emotions, and trust dynamics

### Quantitative Phase — Online Survey
- 50+ respondents
- Mix of multiple choice, Likert scale (1–10), and open-ended questions
- Data cleaning and coding in Excel
- Social Network Analysis (SNA) to visualize trust-based communication clusters

---

## Key Findings

| Finding | Result |
|---|---|
| Rely on others' opinions when buying FMCG | **>80%** of respondents |
| Average reliance on recommendations (scale 1–10) | **5.4 / 10** |
| Most trusted source | **Friends & family** (significantly above online influencers) |
| Experienced purchase regret from not seeking advice | **>70%** of respondents |

### 4 Key Behavioral Patterns Identified

1. **High reliance on WOM** — most respondents rated their dependence at 7–9 out of 10
2. **Friends and family dominate** — mentioned by 35 and 31 respondents respectively, vs. 9 for influencers
3. **Category effect** — WOM is strongest for personal care, health, and hygiene products; weakest for routine food/beverage
4. **Learning from regret** — 64% of respondents began reading reviews more frequently after a bad purchase experience

## Business Implications

- FMCG brands targeting Gen Z should prioritize peer referral programs over influencer campaigns
- Personal care and health products benefit most from authentic user testimonials
- Post-purchase regret is a key behavioral driver — managing negative reviews is critical
